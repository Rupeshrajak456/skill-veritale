# Module 5: Narrative

Part of the Veritale skill. Read `SKILL.md` first. Skip this module in `timeline` mode.

## Purpose

Write a readable story that is honest about its evidence. The aim is a narrative a teacher could read aloud without having to correct it afterwards.

## The core rule

Verified claims carry the plot. Disputed and Legend claims may appear, but only with framing that tells the reader how much to trust them.

## Framing phrases

Use plain past tense for Verified claims. For the other two labels, use phrases like these and vary them so the story does not sound mechanical.

| Label | Phrases that work |
|---|---|
| Verified | (no framing needed) "On 14 October the two armies met near Hastings." |
| Disputed | "Sources disagree about ..."; "According to X ..., while Y says ..."; "Historians still argue over whether ..."; "The figure is uncertain." |
| Legend | "A story first written down by X, some 150 years later, says ..."; "Tradition holds that ..."; "Later Buddhist texts tell how ..."; "There is no early evidence for this, but ..." |

You may also show the label inline in brackets, for example "Harold fell [Verified], perhaps struck by an arrow [Disputed]". Do this when the user asks for labels in the text, or when the audience is students.

## Steps

1. Choose an opening that is true. Start with a Verified event, place or date, not with an invented scene.
2. Follow the timeline order unless a short flashback makes the cause clearer.
3. Give each paragraph one main idea: the background, the turning point, the result, the memory of the event.
4. Introduce Disputed and Legend claims at the point in the story where they belong, with framing.
5. End with what the event changed, using Verified claims, then add one line that points the reader to the uncertainty note.

## Things you must not do

- Do not write dialogue unless a named source records the words. If you quote, name the source.
- Do not describe what people felt, feared or wanted unless a source says so. Write "the edict says Ashoka felt remorse", not "Ashoka wept over the battlefield".
- Do not add weather, sounds, smells or crowd scenes that no source mentions.
- Do not invent minor characters to move the story along.
- Do not resolve a dispute by choosing the more dramatic version.

## Matching the audience

| Audience | Style |
|---|---|
| Students | Short sentences, labels shown inline, one key term explained per paragraph |
| General readers | Framing phrases instead of inline labels, a little more colour from Verified details |
| Specialists | Name the sources more often, give dates of the sources, use standard scholarly terms |

## Output of this module

The story, under the heading "The story", at the length chosen in module 1.
